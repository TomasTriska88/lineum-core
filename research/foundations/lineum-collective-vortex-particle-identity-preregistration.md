## 1. Answer first

A Lineum particle does not have to be one privileged vortex surrounded by a passive wake. It may instead be a persistent organization of many vortices whose individual members can appear, disappear, or exchange while the macroscopic entity preserves its location, shape, motion, internal relations, and response to perturbation.

The everyday picture is a flock of birds. The flock is not one bird plus a trail of other birds. Its identity can reside in stable relations and collective motion even while individual membership changes. In Lineum this analogy generates a testable alternative, but it is not evidence that vortices are agents or that they explicitly execute flocking rules.

The frozen first decision is therefore:

```text
Do not assume that the broad multi-defect structure is a failed particle.
Do not assume that one defect is the particle and the rest are wake.
Test three competing ontologies on the same recovered P2 state:

GAS:       a statistically persistent but non-identifying defect population;
CORE:      one or a few privileged defects plus support and wake;
COLLECTIVE:a bounded, self-maintaining relational organization whose members may turn over.
```

This report is a sibling of the core-envelope-wake preregistration. It does not supersede that report. The two programmes become direct competitors once the exact P2 package is recovered.

## 3. Provenance of the collective-particle hypothesis

The project owner proposed that the apparent particle may be analogous to a flock of birds: simple local interactions among many vortices could produce an emergent structure that behaves as one entity.

The scientifically retained form is:

> A particle candidate may be a bounded collective organization whose identity is carried by persistent macroscopic and relational invariants rather than by permanent membership of one specific defect.

This is intentionally different from four weaker statements:

1. many vortices exist at the same time;
2. a population-average image changes slowly;
3. adjacent frames overlap strongly;
4. a detector repeatedly reports a similar defect count.

Those observations can arise from a gas, frozen disorder, observer smoothing, boundary transport, or replacement without object identity.

The hypothesis becomes stronger only if the organization survives selective interventions and recovers in a way that energy-matched, geometry-matched, and relation-destroyed controls do not.

## 4. Evidence layers at preregistration

### 4.1 What the current implementation computes

The exact active implementation and the exact P2 runner must be SHA-pinned after recovery. At present the relevant model state consists of declared numerical fields and local update rules. Vortices or defects are observer-defined structures detected from that field evolution.

The current implementation does not contain explicit bird-like agents, neighbor lists, alignment commands, separation commands, cohesion commands, a particle identity variable, or a collective repair controller unless later source recovery proves otherwise.

Any apparent flocking rule must therefore be demonstrated as an effective consequence of the field dynamics and observer, not read into the code from the analogy.

### 4.2 What was reproducibly observed in the retained P2 summary

The continuity ledger records a P2 matrix of 168 trajectories across two source families and seven schedules.

For the centered-only formation lane, source-off late multi-defect activity occurred in `12/12` seeds. Reported late-window summaries were:

```text
mean defect count:              20.5232
final-frame mean defect count:  15.6667
mean resolved-core fraction:     0.974687
minimum resolved-core fraction:  0.941535
mean adjacent-frame overlap:     0.99999892
mean orientation:                0.990655
```

The retained population did not pass the whole-movie population rule, and no persistent member identities were assigned. It was therefore classified as a transported multi-defect or vortex-gas remnant rather than a localized particle.

These values justify a collective-identity test because they show strong aggregate continuity. They do not prove a collective particle because aggregate continuity can remain high while individual members are replaced or while a diffuse population is transported.

### 4.3 Cautious interpretation

The P2 result is compatible with at least three possibilities:

```text
H_GAS:
    a non-identifying population with stable summary statistics;

H_CORE:
    one or a few causally privileged defects embedded in a broad support region;

H_COLLECTIVE:
    a bounded relational organization with member turnover and collective repair.
```

### 4.4 Hypothesis and analogy boundary

The flock analogy suggests measurements of membership turnover, relational order, perturbation recovery, splitting, and coherent response. It does not establish cognition, intention, life, agency, selfhood, consciousness, social behavior, or biological equivalence.

### 4.5 Real-physics boundary

A simulation-level collective object is not a Standard Model particle. A physical-particle interpretation would additionally require appropriate symmetries, conserved quantities, dispersion, interactions, scale mapping, stability, quantum statistics where relevant, experimental correspondence, and a discriminating prediction. None is supplied by this preregistration.

## 5. Frozen vocabulary

### 5.1 Member defect

A `member defect` is one connected, observer-detected topological or structure-bearing unit at a frame. Its exact detector must be recovered from P2 and frozen before intervention.

A member is not automatically a particle.

### 5.2 Collective candidate

A `collective candidate` is a bounded set of member defects and the minimum declared field region needed to define their relations at a frame.

The candidate must be detected without using future intervention outcomes.

### 5.3 Membership identity

`Membership identity` means continuity of a particular detected defect through time under a frozen assignment rule.

Member identities may fail while collective identity remains possible.

### 5.4 Collective identity

`Collective identity` means object-level continuity of a bounded macroscopic and relational organization despite limited member turnover.

It requires more than image overlap. It must include:

- bounded localization;
- coherent object-level motion;
- nontrivial internal relational order;
- persistence after source removal;
- selective recovery after perturbation;
- failure of matched non-identifying controls;
- no dependence on an undeclared observer projection.

### 5.5 Relational macrostate

For each candidate at time `t`, define the preregistered macrostate vector:

```text
M(t) = {
    centroid,
    equivalent radius,
    area or occupied support,
    principal-axis orientation,
    shape moments,
    object velocity,
    signed defect inventory,
    total defect count,
    radial pair-correlation profile,
    angular alignment profile,
    coherent transport score,
    far-field disturbance score,
    declared field budgets within the candidate boundary
}
```

Every component must be reported separately. A weighted scalar identity score may be added only as a secondary summary after its weights are frozen from untouched calibration data.

### 5.6 Member turnover

With frozen member matching between adjacent frames:

```text
turnover(t) = 1 - matched_members(t -> t+1) /
                  max(member_count(t), member_count(t+1))
```

The exact treatment of births, deaths, merges, and splits must be declared by the recovered observer or added transparently before interventions.

### 5.7 Collective boundary

The collective boundary is the smallest bounded region that contains the detected members and the frozen relational support required to compute `M(t)`.

At least three boundary observers must be retained:

```text
B0 member hull only;
B1 member hull plus morphology-derived support band;
B2 full matched disturbance region.
```

A result that exists only under B2 is at risk of relabeling the entire environment as the object.

## 6. Observer requirements

The collective observer must not merely count defects or compare full-frame images.

It must separately track:

```text
O_member:
    individual member continuity and turnover;

O_macro:
    collective centroid, boundary, shape, orientation, and velocity;

O_relation:
    pair distances, angular order, signed-defect arrangement,
    and other internal relational descriptors;

O_localization:
    candidate concentration relative to shifted, shuffled,

O_far:
    far-field quietness and domain filling;

O_budget:
    declared field norm, source, sink, transport, boundary flux,
    and intervention work where calculable.
```

A collective identity claim is invalid if it can be reproduced by a frozen smooth patch, translated random texture, a full-domain vortex gas, or an observer whose mask is advected by construction.

- far-field disturbance;
- spontaneous split and merge frequency;

The recovery horizon must be expressed in both model time and object-relative units:

```text
T_cross = 2 * R_eq / max(v_object, epsilon)

T_test  = max(2 * T_cross, 2 * T_corr)
```

If the object speed is too small for a meaningful `T_cross`, use the correlation-based horizon and report the stationary classification.

All baseline predictive intervals and normalization scales are frozen before intervention outputs are opened.

## 8. Frozen null families

Every intervention requires paired controls from the same pre-intervention state.

### N0 untouched continuation

No edit. This is the counterfactual reference.

### N1 sham edit

Run the same extraction, serialization, masking, and reinsertion operations without changing values.

### N2 energy-matched random removal

Remove the same declared budget as the targeted member intervention from random cells outside the candidate while preserving the intervention algorithm.

### N3 geometry-matched wrong region

Apply the same mask geometry upstream, downstream, translated, or rotated away from the candidate.

### N4 member-count-preserving relation shuffle

Preserve the number and signed inventory of detected members, but destroy their original spatial relations within a matched envelope.

### N5 macro-envelope-preserving field randomization

Preserve the coarse candidate envelope and declared total budgets while randomizing fine internal phase or structure using a method compatible with the recovered state representation.

### N6 smooth transported null

Construct or recover an energy-matched smooth patch whose centroid and envelope are advected similarly but which lacks the candidate internal topology.

A test lane is not identifying unless it separates the candidate from the applicable nulls.

## 9. Frozen intervention matrix

All interventions begin from saved, identical pre-intervention states and continue under the unchanged recovered P2 equation, source-off schedule, parameters, RNG policy, boundary conditions, and observer.

### I0 untouched paired continuation

Continue the state with no edit for the full `T_test` horizon.

### I1 random member ablation

Remove randomly selected detected members at fractions:

```text
10%, 20%, and 40% of the pre-intervention member count.
```

Removal must replace the member-local declared state with a matched local baseline, not with an arbitrary zero unless zero is the recovered baseline.

Questions:

- Does the collective boundary remain localized?
- Does the missing population regenerate?
- Do relational profiles return toward the untouched continuation?
- Does object motion continue within baseline predictive bounds?

### I2 targeted structural ablation

Remove members selected by preregistered structural roles:

- nearest the centroid;
- farthest from the centroid;
- highest local relational centrality;
- one sign of winding where both signs exist;
- leading-edge or trailing-edge members relative to motion.

This separates a distributed collective from a privileged core or directional engine.

### I3 member displacement

Translate selected members and their minimum local support by frozen distances:

```text
0.5 R_eq and 1.0 R_eq
```

in transverse and longitudinal directions, using seam-safe handling under periodic boundaries.

Questions:

- Do displaced members return?
- Does the collective reorganize around the displacement?
- Does the object preserve motion while membership changes?

### I4 relation-destroying microstate shuffle

Preserve, as closely as the recovered state allows:

- member count;
- signed defect inventory;
- coarse envelope;
- declared total field budgets.

Destroy the original pairwise and angular relations.

If the original relation pattern is causally important, the shuffled state should fail or reconstruct more slowly than sham and untouched controls.

### I5 collective bisection

Cut the candidate across its minor and major principal axes, producing two separated halves while preserving each half's local state.

Classify outcomes:

```text
one half dies and the other repairs;
both repair into two candidates;
halves rejoin;
both disperse;
only a privileged member-bearing half survives.
```

This test is executed only after I1-I4 establish a stable observer and non-destructive editing method.

### I6 matched collective interaction

Bring two recovered candidates into a matched encounter only after single-candidate identity gates pass.

Possible outcomes to record without interpretation inflation:

- elastic-like deflection;
- merger;
- fragmentation;
- passage through;
- exchange of members;
- loss of localization;
- boundary artifact.

This is a later lane and is not required for the first collective-identity decision.

### I7 core-envelope-wake cross-test

Apply the competing sibling decomposition to the same state:

- retain only the proposed privileged core;
- retain core plus minimum support;
- retain the remainder without the proposed core;
- transplant each under matched controls.

This directly distinguishes `H_CORE` from `H_COLLECTIVE` rather than assuming one ontology.

### 10.4 Repair and recovery

For each macrostate component `j`, normalize distance from the untouched counterfactual using the frozen baseline scale `s_j`:

```text
d_j(t) = abs(M_j_intervention(t) - M_j_control(t)) / max(s_j, epsilon)
```

Define component recovery only when `d_j(t)` returns inside the frozen untouched predictive interval and remains there for a continuous confirmation window.

Report the fraction of components recovered, but do not hide failed components in an average.

A secondary repair fraction may be reported as:

```text
repair_fraction_j = 1 - d_j(recovery_window) / d_j(immediate_post_edit)
```

with zero-denominator and overshoot handling declared explicitly.

## 11. Frozen decision rules

### 11.1 Collective particle candidate supported

Classify as `CP2 self-repairing collective candidate` only if all of the following pass:

1. the candidate remains bounded and source-off over the declared horizon;
2. object-level centroid and motion continuity outperform smooth and shuffled nulls;
3. limited random member ablation produces recovery of a preregistered majority of macrostate and relational components;
4. recovery is stronger than sham-corrected, energy-matched, and wrong-region controls;
5. collective continuity persists despite nonzero member turnover;
6. relation-destroying shuffles materially impair or delay reconstruction relative to sham controls;
7. no one privileged member is necessary across seeds unless the classification is changed to `H_CORE`;
8. the result survives observer, boundary, and domain-size controls;
9. intervention accounting does not explain recovery as injected replacement or hidden source;
10. the full evidence remains stronger than adjacent-frame overlap alone.

This classification remains simulation-internal and does not authorize the word `particle` without the qualifier `collective candidate` in scientific claims.

### 11.2 Macro-persistent but non-repairing collective

Classify as `CP1 macro-persistent organization` when aggregate structure and motion persist, but selective perturbations do not produce relation-specific recovery beyond controls.

This is stronger than a raw gas statistic but weaker than a self-maintaining particle candidate.

### 11.3 Vortex gas supported

Classify as `CP0 non-identifying vortex gas` when:

- population statistics persist but bounded object continuity fails;
- apparent identity is reproduced by smooth, shuffled, or transported nulls;
- perturbations disperse or replace the population without selective recovery;
- relational descriptors carry no predictive or causal information;
- observer masks create continuity by construction.

### 11.4 Privileged-core model supported

Classify as `CP3 privileged-core dependent` when survival and recovery reproducibly depend on one or a small subset of special members, while removing comparable non-core members has materially less effect.

The next programme then returns to core-envelope-wake decomposition.

### 11.5 Observer artifact

Classify as `CP4 observer-dependent artifact` when the conclusion changes materially across reasonable member, boundary, assignment, or localization observers and no observer is independently identifying against nulls.

### 11.6 Inconclusive

Classify as inconclusive rather than tuning parameters when:

- exact P2 state recovery fails;
- the editor injects uncontrolled state;
- the detector cannot distinguish births, deaths, merges, and splits;
- baseline candidate boundaries are not stable enough to freeze;
- the horizon is too short relative to baseline correlation time;
- required nulls cannot be constructed fairly.

## 12. Negative-result and repair protocol

A failed collective test must record:

1. the exact failed gate and scope;
2. any aggregate behavior that remained positive;
3. whether failure belongs to equation, state, observer, boundary, resolution, intervention, or interpretation;
4. whether one privileged core survived;
5. at least three distinct repair classes when scientifically plausible;
6. the cheapest frozen discriminator among them.

Blind parameter tuning is forbidden after a decision-relevant negative result. The project owner receives the smallest useful evidence set and is then invited to provide a lay, creative interpretation before a replacement mechanism is selected.
