## 1. Answer first


A simple picture is a river. Fast ripples, a persistent current, and the long-term shape of the riverbed can look like three different things. They may nevertheless be three descriptions of one water-flow process at different timescales. In the same way, `Phi` may carry the fast environmental response while `mu` is only the accumulated or coarse-grained form of that response.

The report therefore does not begin by asking what new power `mu` should receive. It begins with the stricter question:

```text
Can every causal and predictive effect assigned to mu be reconstructed from a sufficiently complete Psi/Phi history?
```

If yes, `mu` is an effective description, memory coordinate, or computational convenience rather than a proven independent physical ingredient. If no, the remaining difference must be demonstrated by a frozen intervention or prediction before a richer `mu` state is considered.

```text

hierarchical branching across scales;
statistical self-similarity;

```

No one quantum interpretation is privileged. Copenhagen-family, Everettian, Bohmian, objective-collapse, relational, consistent-histories, QBist, and other distinguishable families remain live comparison lanes until a Lineum mechanism produces an empirical discriminator.

## 8. Reduction-first `mu` versus `Phi` hypothesis family

The emergence-first and minimal-ingredient gate requires the following variants to be tested before introducing a richer fundamental state.

This is the implemented baseline. It does not decide whether the same behavior could be represented without an explicit `mu` coordinate.

### 8.2 Variant R1: temporally integrated `Phi`

A candidate reduction is:

```text
mu_hat(x,t) = integral from -infinity to t of K(t-s) Phi(x,s) ds
```

where `K` is a declared memory kernel.

Equivalent discrete form:

```text
mu_hat[t+1] = lambda mu_hat[t] + (1-lambda) Phi[t+1]
```

This variant says that `mu` is not a new phenomenon. It is the retained history of `Phi`.

The exact kernel, normalization, units, and boundary behavior must be fitted on training trajectories and frozen before out-of-sample comparison.

### 8.3 Variant R2: coarse-grained or higher-scale `Phi`

A candidate reduction is:

```text
mu_hat = C_(ell,tau)[Phi]
```

where `C_(ell,tau)` coarse-grains `Phi` over spatial scale `ell` and temporal scale `tau`.

This is the closest operational translation of the owner's statement that `mu` and `Phi` may be the same phenomenon at different levels.

Required checks:

- convergence across spatial and temporal resolution;
- stability of the mapping under changes of `ell` and `tau`;
- preservation of causal ordering;
- whether the same reduced variable predicts future `Psi` as well as explicit `mu`;
- whether the mapping survives interventions rather than only passive curve fitting.

### 8.4 Variant R3: joint `Psi/Phi` history embedding

Because the current code writes `mu` from `Psi` activity, a fair reduction cannot test only `Phi`.

A broader candidate is:

```text
mu_hat(t) = H[Psi(s), Phi(s) for s <= t]
```

where `H` is a frozen finite-memory or state-reconstruction map.

If this map reproduces all future effects of explicit `mu`, then `mu` may be a Markovian state coordinate that compresses the non-Markovian history of `Psi/Phi`, not an additional physical substance.

### 8.5 Variant R4: redundant reparameterization

In the strongest reduction:

```text

```

Then `mu` adds no independently identifiable causal degree of freedom. It may remain useful computationally, but ontology must not be inferred from it.

### 8.6 Variant R5: genuinely independent `mu`

A separate `mu` role is justified only if a frozen test shows at least one of the following:

- two states with matched complete accessible `Psi/Phi` histories but different `mu` produce reliably different futures;
- an intervention on `mu` changes an observable in a way no admissible `Psi/Phi` history model reproduces;
- `mu` contains predictive information not reconstructible from the allowed `Psi/Phi` history;
- removing `mu` breaks a declared conservation, causal, or state-identification account;
- a richer `mu` model makes a new empirical prediction and survives its null controls.

The burden of proof is on R5, not on R1-R4.

## 9. Coupling is not entanglement

A local feedback loop can be written as:

```text
Psi(x,t) -> mu(x,t) -> Psi(x,t + dt)
```

This is coupling, but it is not quantum entanglement.

Entanglement requires a joint state that cannot be fully represented as independent states of its parts. A branch-aware Lineum hypothesis would need to encode correlations among alternatives or separated subsystems, not merely a scalar trace at one coordinate.

A minimal bookkeeping scaffold is:

```text
Omega = sum_b c_b |Psi_b, Phi_b, mu_b>
```

where `b` labels an alternative description and `c_b` is a candidate amplitude or weight. This is not derived from the current PDE and is not a canonical Lineum equation.

## 12. Is effective universe branching fractal?

Branching is not automatically fractal.

### 12.1 Variant B0: tree-like only

### 12.2 Variant B1: hierarchical branching

Branch-like differentiation repeats at several descriptive scales, but the statistics may change with scale.

### 12.3 Variant B2: statistically self-similar branching

After rescaling, selected branch statistics approximately repeat over a declared finite range.

Candidate observables:

- branch-count distribution;
- branch-weight distribution;
- persistence time;
- similarity of subtrees;
- record divergence;
- recombination accessibility.

### 12.4 Variant B3: mathematical fractal

A genuine fractal claim requires a stable scaling relation such as:

```text
N(epsilon) proportional to epsilon^(-D)
```

where `N(epsilon)` is the number of distinguishable occupied boxes or branch features at resolution `epsilon`, and `D` is a converged dimension over a declared scaling range.

Required gates:

- at least one decade of credible scaling, preferably more;
- resolution and finite-size convergence;
- stability across seeds and observer definitions;
- comparison with random trees, multiplicative cascades, critical branching, and non-fractal hierarchical nulls;
- preregistered fitting range;
- uncertainty on `D`;
- no inference from visual resemblance alone.

### 12.5 Operational caution

In Everettian quantum mechanics, branches are generally emergent and approximate rather than uniquely countable elementary objects. A branch-count fractal dimension can therefore be observer- and coarse-graining-dependent. Any Lineum fractal claim must specify the branch observer before measuring scaling.

## 16. Hard compatibility gates with established quantum evidence

### 16.1 Bell correlations

A purely local scalar `mu` carrying predetermined answers cannot reproduce the full class of observed Bell correlations. A viable model must be nonlocal, contextual, relational, branch-based, or otherwise outside the rejected local factorization while remaining empirically adequate.

### 16.2 No controllable superluminal signalling

Local interventions at A must not change the outcome distribution observable at B alone. Joint correlations may depend on both settings, but distant marginals must remain invariant within experimental bounds.

### 16.3 Decoherence and environmental records

A viable `Phi` interpretation must reproduce that distinguishable environmental records suppress interference without human awareness and that sufficiently controlled removal of distinguishing information can restore conditional interference.

Decoherence alone must not be silently presented as a derivation of a unique experienced outcome or the Born rule.

### 16.4 Quantum eraser

If `mu` permanently stores accessible which-path information, interference should remain suppressed after ordinary `Phi` records are erased.

A viable model must choose and test one of the following:

```text
A. mu does not store path identity before an irreversible record forms;
B. mu path records can be coherently uncomputed with Phi;
C. mu stores only inaccessible global correlations;
D. mu predicts residual interference loss beyond standard quantum mechanics
```

Option D is a new empirical prediction and requires comparison with interferometry bounds.

### 16.5 Objective-collapse bounds

Any stochastic or nonlinear collapse term must declare its rate, localization scale, energy cost, and known experimental constraints. It cannot be inserted merely to force one branch to survive.

### 16.6 Energy and information accounting

Every proposed `mu`, `Phi`, collapse, or cross-branch mechanism must state:

```text
what is stored;
how it is written;
how it feeds back;
what is conserved or dissipated;
what a local observer can access;
where apparently lost coherence or information resides;
whether the update injects norm or energy
```

## 21. Support, narrowing, and falsification

### 21.1 Support for higher-scale identity of `mu` and `Phi`

Support requires:

- a frozen `Phi` or joint-history reduction;
- out-of-sample prediction comparable to explicit `mu`;
- intervention-level equivalence;
- stable mapping across declared scales;
- no hidden target leakage;
- simpler or equally justified state description.

### 21.2 Support for independent `mu`

Support requires:

- a causal effect not reproduced by admissible `Psi/Phi` history models;
- closed accounting;
- robustness across resolution and seeds;
- a preregistered new prediction or indispensable state-identification role;
- independent reproduction.

### 21.3 Support for branch-relative identity

Support requires a simple observer register whose pre-branch shared history and post-branch divergence are operationally measurable. Exact state replay, copied labels, or visual resemblance do not prove identity.

### 21.4 Support for fractality

Support requires a converged scaling law and rejection of non-fractal tree and cascade nulls. Repeated visual branching is insufficient.

### 21.5 Narrowing or falsification

The richer `mu` family is narrowed if every effect is reproduced by ordinary local memory, delayed or coarse-grained `Phi`, or joint `Psi/Phi` history.

The branch-aware family is narrowed if it fails Bell, no-signalling, probability, eraser, or accounting gates.

The fractal family is narrowed if scaling exponents drift with resolution, observer, threshold, or finite size and no stable range survives null comparison.

A bounded negative result falsifies only the tested implementation and domain. It does not falsify every metaphysical interpretation.
