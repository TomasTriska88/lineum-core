## 6. The central distinction: coupling is not entanglement

A local feedback loop can be written as:

```text
Psi(x,t) -> mu(x,t) -> Psi(x,t + dt)
```

This is coupling, but it is not quantum entanglement.

Entanglement requires a joint state that cannot be fully represented as independent states of its parts. In a branch-aware Lineum hypothesis, the relevant object would need to encode correlations among alternatives or separated subsystems, not merely a scalar trace at one coordinate.

A minimal conceptual joint-state notation is:

```text
Omega = sum_b c_b |Psi_b, Phi_b, mu_b>
```

where:

- `b` labels a branch-relative alternative, not a new physical claim;
- `c_b` is a complex branch amplitude or another future weight variable;
- `Psi_b` is the active configuration relative to branch `b`;
- `Phi_b` is the environment/measurement record relative to branch `b`;
- `mu_b` is the slow continuity state relative to branch `b`.

This expression is only a bookkeeping scaffold. It is not derived from the current PDE and is not authorized as a canonical Lineum equation.

## 9. The probability problem

The phrase "ego sees the most probable branch" is not sufficient.

Suppose two outcomes have weights:


A rule that every observer always sees only branch A would predict no observers in branch B and would not reproduce repeated quantum frequencies. A viable model must instead explain why repeated branch-relative experiences follow the observed weight distribution.

Required alternatives include, but are not limited to:

1. a derivation of outcome weights equivalent to `|c_b|^2`;
2. a self-location measure over branch-relative ego instances;
3. a dynamical selection law that reproduces the same statistics without controllable bias;
4. a demonstrably different probability rule with a new empirical prediction.

Until such a derivation exists:

```text

most_probable_branch_only = rejected_as_inadequate
```

## 10. Hard compatibility gates with established quantum evidence

### 10.1 Bell correlations

Bell's theorem rules out broad classes of local hidden-variable explanations for quantum correlations.

Consequences for this hypothesis:

- a purely local scalar `mu` carrying predetermined answers cannot reproduce all quantum correlations;
- a viable `mu` mechanism would need to be nonlocal, contextual, relational, branch-based, or otherwise outside the rejected local factorization;
- matching Bell correlations is necessary but not sufficient evidence for the hypothesis.

### 10.2 No controllable superluminal signalling

Even if `mu` contains global or nonlocal correlations, local interventions must not allow one observer to alter the distant observer's marginal outcome statistics in a controllable way.

Required test:

```text
change local measurement setting or local mu intervention at A;
verify that the outcome distribution observable at B alone remains unchanged;
allow only joint correlations to change according to the model
```

Any proposed cross-branch `mu` equation that enables controllable faster-than-light communication fails this gate unless it explicitly predicts and survives corresponding experimental tests.

### 10.3 Decoherence and environmental records

Environment-induced decoherence explains how stable branch-relative records can emerge through correlations with the environment. A Lineum `Phi` interpretation must reproduce at least the relevant operational facts:

- distinguishable environment records suppress observable interference;
- no human awareness is required;
- sufficiently controlled removal of distinguishing information can restore conditional interference;
- decoherence alone does not automatically derive a unique experienced outcome or the Born rule.

### 10.4 Quantum eraser constraint

If `mu` permanently stores which-branch information in an accessible way, interference should remain suppressed even after the ordinary `Phi` record is erased. Quantum-eraser phenomena therefore create a discriminating gate.

A viable branch-aware `mu` model must choose one of the following and predict the consequences:

```text
A. mu does not store path identity before an irreversible branch record forms;
B. mu path records can be coherently uncomputed together with Phi;
C. mu stores correlations globally but no local branch-accessible which-path record;
D. mu predicts residual interference loss beyond standard quantum mechanics
```

Option D would be a genuinely new empirical prediction and must be compared with existing interferometry.

### 10.5 Born-rule gate

The model must reproduce outcome frequencies. Descriptive words such as probability, significance, resonance, ego preference, or branch thickness are not a derivation.

### 10.6 Energy and information accounting

Cross-branch or global `mu` coupling must not silently inject active `Psi` norm, delete entropy, or copy information without a declared ledger.

Required accounting:

```text
what is stored in mu;
how it is written;
how it feeds back;
which quantity is conserved or dissipated;
what local observer can access;
what branch information is unavailable;
where apparent information loss goes
```
