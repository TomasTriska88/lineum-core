## 3. Historical and source-critical constraints

### 3.1 No synthetic ancient system

The sources remain historically distinct:

```text
Stoic pneuma physics;
Sethian and related Nag Hammadi creation narratives;
Valentinian pairing, Limit, ritual, image, and restoration language;
1 Enoch and related Enochic literature;
Qumran sectarian, wisdom, calendrical, and liturgical manuscripts.
```

A similar structural motif does not prove contact, transmission, or shared doctrine.

### 3.2 Manuscript plurality is evidence, not noise

The Community Rule exists in a complete Cave 1 copy and multiple related Cave 4 and Cave 5 manuscripts. Current critical work presents the manuscripts separately because their clustered differences can represent distinct understandings and stages of development rather than mere copying errors.

The Two Spirits instruction itself has a literary history. A 2021 Brill study reconstructs a core and several expansions, while a 2024 Oxford commentary notes that the instruction differs in subject and vocabulary from the association rules and may have originated outside the association before being received into the Rule.

Therefore this report must not speak of `the Qumran protocol` as one immutable byte string.

### 3.3 Valentinian reconstruction is source-sensitive

Descriptions of paired aeons, Limit/Cross, Sophia, and Pleroma vary among ancient reports and surviving texts. A Cambridge study on gnostic soteriology notes that Irenaeus attributes two Limit/Cross figures to one version of Valentinian teaching, while Hippolytus gives another sequence. No single later handbook reconstruction may be treated as a verbatim universal Valentinian system.

### 3.4 Calendrical interpretation remains disputed

Scholarship increasingly describes the 364-day tradition as schematic rather than simply `solar`. Intercalation remains debated, and recent scholarship still notes the lack of explicit textual evidence for a correction procedure. This is useful as a methodological comparison, not as a verdict about what every Qumran group practiced.

## 5. Dynamic boundary: separation is not enough

### 5.1 Textual and historical coordinates

Valentinian sources and ancient reports assign a `Limit` or `Limit/Cross` role at a boundary of the Pleroma. The reconstructions differ, but two functions are repeatedly relevant in later summaries and source discussions:

```text
separation or exclusion of what does not belong;
stabilization or establishment of what remains within.
```

The Songs of the Sabbath Sacrifice offer a distinct Jewish liturgical image. Their heavenly-temple architecture is not always inert scenery: walls, corners, carved beings, wheels, and other architectural elements participate in praise. Noam Mizrahi describes the cycle as a thirteen-song progression inward through the modeled heavenly temple, and Ra'anan Boustan analyzes the poetics of angels in the architecture.

These are unrelated traditions for present purposes. They converge only as a systems question:

> Can a boundary be part of the organized dynamics rather than a passive line around them?

### 5.2 Structural abstraction

A functional boundary can have at least five separable roles:

```text
B0 geometric separator:
    marks inside and outside but has no state or causal response;

B1 selective gate:
    allows some fluxes and blocks others;

B2 receiving interface:
    stores or transforms incoming flux rather than deleting it;

B3 stabilizing boundary:
    supplies restoring feedback that preserves an interior mode;

B4 active participant:
    joins the collective dynamics and can change state, fail, recover, or synchronize;

B5 hidden pump:
    appears stabilizing but injects undeclared energy or information.
```

These roles are observationally confusable if the observer records only whether the interior survives.

## 6. Image-copy versus causal-state reproduction

### 6.1 Textual and historical coordinates

In the Hypostasis of the Archons, rulers model a human from earth after their own form and after an image seen in water. The modeled figure is not initially able to function as the completed living human of the narrative; an additional spirit or life principle enters. The text is a theological rewriting of Genesis, not a scientific account of copying.

The Gospel of Philip repeatedly distinguishes names, images, visible rites, and their hidden or higher referents. It also warns that worldly names can misdirect the hearer from what they claim to name. Modern work on the text emphasizes ritual transformation and reunification rather than a simple informational codebook.

## 10. Internal exactness versus external calibration

### 10.1 Textual and scholarly coordinates

The Astronomical Book and Qumran calendrical traditions organize time through exact courses, portals, weeks, seasons, and a 364-day schematic year. The attraction of 364 is structural: it is exactly divisible into fifty-two weeks and four thirteen-week quarters, keeping dates on stable weekdays.

Modern scholarship continues to debate practice and intercalation. A 2008 survey by Jonathan Ben-Dov and Stéphane Saulnier stresses the schematic character of the tradition. A 2026 Harvard Theological Review article notes that the 364-day year falls short of the tropical year by about 1.24 days annually and that no explicit Qumran intercalation procedure has been recovered; the historical interpretation remains disputed.

### 10.2 Structural abstraction

A model can be internally exact, elegant, periodic, and socially reproducible while drifting from an external process.

```text
internal closure != empirical calibration
```

### 10.3 Lineum relevance

This is a programme-wide warning for:

- internally consistent simulation laws;
- source schedules and resonances;
- phase locking;
- whitepaper equations;
- numerical conservation in the wrong model;
- engineered copying ledgers;
- cosmological analogies;
- claims of correspondence with real particles or gravity.

A green test proves conformance to the declared test, not truth about nature.
